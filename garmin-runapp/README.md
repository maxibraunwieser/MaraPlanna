# MaraPlanna (Garmin Connect IQ device app)

A run recording app with the target paces from MaraPlanna. The texts on the watch are German.

- **Home screen:** the app opens on a watch-face-like screen. **Top:** the weather (symbol and temperature, from the Garmin weather on the watch, in the watch's temperature unit) and the date. **Left:** the MaraPlanna mark with the name under it (while the watch is not linked, the 6-digit pairing code appears below the name). **Right:** the time, hour over minute, in the watch's 12 or 24 hour format. **Bottom:** heart rate, kilometres run this week, and VO2max. Nothing on this screen is typed in: the heart rate is the live sensor value (or the last value of the watch from the past five minutes), the VO2max is the running value from the Garmin profile, the weather is the Garmin weather (older than six hours it is hidden), and the weekly kilometres come from MaraPlanna and from the runs this app recorded (see below). A value the watch does not have shows as `--` (a grey symbol); the weather is left out. START, a tap or the menu button (long press UP) opens the menu, BACK closes the app.
- **Menu:** four entries, each with a live subtitle. 1) **Training** (subtitle: today's session): today's MaraPlanna session first (with "Heute · 7,1 km · 37 min", and "Erledigt" if MaraPlanna already shows it as done), then the preset run types Easy, Long, Recovery, Marathon, Threshold, Interval and Repetition with their pace range (only the ones MaraPlanna or the app settings have a pace for), then Freier Lauf. Choosing one opens the **ready screen**. 2) **Absolviert** (subtitle: "23,0 / 35,1 km", the kilometres of the week): a list, newest first. The first row, "Diese Woche", opens the week view (kilometres run and planned with a bar, and every weekday with its status: a green tick for done, a ring for planned, orange today, red if the day has passed without it, a cross for skipped). Below it come the sessions done recently: the runs of the last four weeks and the last strength trainings, each with date and distance. Pressing START or tapping one opens its **details**: distance (and the planned distance), time, average pace, average heart rate, climb and training effect for a run, or one line per exercise ("3 × 8 · 60 kg") for a strength training; UP/DOWN or a swipe scrolls. A run this app has just saved shows up at once (the app keeps its own last six runs) and is replaced by the server's entry as soon as MaraPlanna knows the run through Garmin Connect (same start time within five minutes), so it never shows twice. 3) **Trainingsplan** (subtitle: "90 Tage bis zum Rennen" or "Nicht gekoppelt"): not linked: the pairing code and where to enter it. Linked: every session of this and next week as a row (title, date, distance) with a status symbol (green tick done, orange ring today, grey ring planned, red ring missed, cross skipped); pressing START or tapping one opens the **steps** of the session: warm-up, the interval block ("5 × 1000 m" with its target pace and the jog recovery), cool-down, or the exercises of a strength day. The last row, "Rennen & Stand", shows the days to the goal race and its name, the goal time and VDOT, and when the data was last loaded; START or a tap there loads it again. 4) **Einstellungen**, see below.
- **Design (same colours as the MaraPlanna app):** all screens sit on the night background of the website (`0C0D12`, on AMOLED watches with the soft blue and orange glow at the top). There are **no boxes** on the watch: grey cards made the screens with many numbers hard to read. Things are told apart by thin lines, spacing and colour instead: lists are rows with a coloured stripe (green for easy runs, blue for long runs, orange for the key session, red for hard intervals, violet for strength) and a hairline between rows, the row in focus has a thick orange stripe and white text, the others are grey; tabs are text with an orange underline; the 2×2 overview and the pace comparison are divided by thin lines; page names are orange and the page dots are small pills. Switches look like the website's (green on, grey off); the colours are the website's (orange `F97316`, green `34D399`, red `F87171`, blue `60A5FA`, violet `A78BFA`). The menus are drawn by the app itself (`source/views/CardMenu.mc`) instead of the system menu, so Main menu, Training, Settings, Datenseiten, the pause menus, the strength library and the lists behind Absolviert / Trainingsplan all look the same.
- **Training menu with tabs:** under **Training** three tabs: **Heute** (today's sessions of the plan, chosen directly: the run, and each strength session of the day; with nothing planned a card says so), **Laufen** (all run types with their pace range, and Freier Lauf) and **Kraft** (all strength sessions of today and Kraft frei with the muscle groups; the tab is left out on watches without strength). Swipe left/right or tap a tab to switch; with buttons press UP on the first card to reach the tab bar, START shows the next tab, DOWN goes back into the list.
- **Ready screen:** the chosen session at the top (**HEUTE GEPLANT** with title, distance and duration, or the run type with its pace range). The start button shows the GPS state without any text: orange rings pulse outward while GPS is searched, a calm green ring means GPS is ready. You can start without a GPS fix, e.g. on a treadmill. At the top the heart rate. The dots show which entry is chosen (UP/DOWN or swipe changes it), START begins, BACK goes back to the menu.
- **Settings** (all saved on the watch, each shows its current value): **Datenseiten** (which pages appear during the run), **Pace-Glättung** (how many seconds the current pace is averaged over: 10 s, 20 s (the default) or 30 s), **Einheiten** (Automatisch = the unit chosen in MaraPlanna, or the watch's unit without MaraPlanna; Kilometer; Meilen; applies everywhere at once), **km-Rückblick** (the short summary after each kilometre or mile), **Pace-Alarm** (vibration and tone when the pace leaves the target range), **Töne** (the beeps; a beep briefly interrupts music playing over Bluetooth) and **Vibration** (does not disturb music), each on top of the watch's own settings), **Wechsel-Countdown** (five vibrations, one per second, before every step change of a session; see below), **Wetter anzeigen** (on the home screen), **MaraPlanna trennen** (forget the link and the loaded data; the watch gets a new code at once, with a confirmation first).
- **Current pace:** the speed the GPS chip measures itself (`Activity.Info.currentSpeed`, Doppler based) averaged over the chosen window, not the distance covered in a few seconds. Distance over a short window jumps a lot because a GPS error of a few metres is large against 30 m: in the simulator the shown pace jumped by up to 34 s/km from one second to the next with the old 10 s distance window, and by at most 1.1 s/km with the 10 s speed average (the simulator's speed values are smoother than real GPS, so on a watch the gain is smaller than that, but the direction is the same). **Intervals:** every new workout step and every lap (BACK) restarts the smoothing at zero, so the pace of the interval never mixes with the jog before it. From then on the shown pace is the average of the time since the start until the window (20 s by default) is full, then it slides on as usual; there is a value from the second second. The first 4 seconds after a restart (at most half of the time so far) are left out of the average: the runner is still accelerating there and they would pull the average too slow until the window is full. Without steps, a lasting change (the last 6 seconds differ by more than 12 % from the 20 before, e.g. a sprint, or stopping) restarts the window by itself (not in the first 30 s after a step change: that change is known already). **The watch's own speed lags:** `currentSpeed` is smoothed by the watch itself, so after a jog it still carries the jog pace for several seconds (on 12 Nov a rep showed a far too slow pace 20 s in). As long as the window still holds seconds from the first 12 after a restart, the app therefore takes the distance covered since the restart (after the run-up, from 4 s of distance on) instead of the speed; that distance belongs to the new part only. In the unit test with a watch speed lagging by a 6 s time constant (jog 6:30, rep 4:00): 10 s into the rep the watch speed alone says 4:23, the app shows 4:00; 20 s in: 4:04 against 4:00; from 8 s on the app is never more than 2 % off. Measured on a simulated interval run (3:45 reps, 5:50 jogs, GPS speed with lag and noise): 8 s into a rep the shown pace is within about 2 % of the rep pace (a fixed 10 s window: 13 %, a fixed 20 s window without restart: 32 %), 12 s in it is within 1.3 % (fixed 20 s: 19 %); steady running stays as calm as a fixed 20 s window (standard deviation 4.7 s/km, against 7.3 s/km for 10 s). The measurement is a simulation, not real GPS data; the first 3 seconds of a rep are about 14 % off for every method because the runner has not reached the pace yet. Without a speed value from the watch the app falls back to the distance over the same window. Standing still (below 0.5 m/s) shows `--:--`. The pace alarm only fires after the pace has been outside the target range for 8 seconds in a row, so a short outlier does not trigger it (still at most every 30 s, and not in the first 20 s of a step).
- **Weekly kilometres:** the larger of two values. MaraPlanna's value covers every run of the week, also from other devices, once Garmin Connect has synced it to MaraPlanna. The app's own tally counts the runs it saved this week at once, before MaraPlanna knows them. Taking the larger value (instead of adding) avoids counting a run twice when MaraPlanna has caught up. Without data from either, the value shows `--`.
- **During the run:** ten data pages, refreshed every second, with a clean frame: a red dot (recording) or pause bars at the top, a hint line below the page (LANGSAMER, SCHNELLER, KEIN GPS, pause) and page dots at the bottom. Pages that make no sense right now (the interval pages without a session) are skipped.

  | Page | Shows |
  |---|---|
  | Übersicht | time, distance, pace, heart rate |
  | Pace | the current pace big, a scale with the target range (green) and a dot for you, the deviation in seconds |
  | Puls | heart rate big, zone 1-5 from your Garmin profile as a segmented bar, average and maximum |
  | Pace-Vergleich | three paces at a glance: the current pace large on top, below it the pace of the last kilometer (last mile) on the left and the average pace of the whole run on the right. In a session the left field shows the last finished interval instead (green in range, orange too fast, blue too slow) until the first one is done |
  | Pace-Verlauf | a line chart of the pace over the whole run, with the target range as a band on runs without a session |
  | Puls-Verlauf | a line chart of the heart rate over the whole run |
  | Intervall | your heart rate in small type at the top, then the current step with what is left (m, km or time), a progress bar, the current pace and the step's target pace centred below it |
  | Intervall-Reps | the results of the session at a glance, one row per hard interval (not warm-up, recoveries or cool-down): average pace, how far it is from the target range ("OK" or seconds too fast/slow) and average heart rate. Finished reps are coloured green (in range), orange (too fast) or blue (too slow), the running rep is highlighted with its progress and a provisional result, reps still to come show how long they are (e.g. "1 km", "400 m") and their target pace, the running one does not (its remaining distance is on the Intervall page). With many reps the list scrolls around the running one |
  | Karte | the route so far, the start, your position and an arrow back to the start with its distance |
  | Prognose | with a session: planned time, expected finish time and whether you are on target; without: 5 km, 10 km, half marathon and marathon at your average pace, the marathon against your goal time |

- **End screen after saving:** up to five pages, flipped with UP/DOWN, a swipe or a tap (START and BACK close the app). 1) the route you ran on the left (start green, finish white with a red rim) and time, distance and average pace on the right; 2) the heart rate over the whole run as a chart with a dashed line at the average, the average and the maximum, and the start and end time under it; 3) the pace over the whole run with a dashed average line and the average pace; 4) the time in each of the five heart-rate zones as bars (zone 5 on top; the zones come from your Garmin profile and are counted every second of the recording). 5) the Training Effect: the aerobic value comes from the watch (Activity.Info.trainingEffect, 1.0 to 5.0, read just before saving) with its rating (Kein Effekt, Geringer Effekt, Erhaltend, Verbessernd, Stark verbessernd, Überlastung) and a five-step bar. Connect IQ does not give apps the anaerobic Training Effect, so the page shows **its own estimate**, labelled "ANAEROB", below it (the label does not say "estimated", but it is). This is not Garmin's number and will not match Garmin Connect. Model (Anaerobic.mc): every second faster than your threshold pace counts, weighted by speed (0 at threshold pace, 1 at interval pace, 2 at repetition pace, 3 at 10 % faster; the three paces come from MaraPlanna), and the sum is mapped to 0 to 5 with a saturating curve (63 % of 5 at 1200 weighted seconds). Easy runs, long runs and threshold runs give 0. The curve is an assumption, not calibrated against Garmin: strides give about 1, 3 x 1000 m at interval pace about 2.3, ten hard 400 m about 4. Without the three paces there is no estimate; the aerobic block is left out on watches that give no value. The two heart-rate pages only appear if a heart rate was recorded. The charts have a fixed size like the data pages: on a long run neighbouring points are merged. The route is the same breadcrumb trail as on the map page.
- **Heart-rate bar on the overview:** below the four values the overview shows the five heart-rate zones as a bar (zones from your Garmin profile). The current zone is lit and taller, the others are dimmed in their zone colour, and a white dot shows where in the zone your heart rate is. The pulse page keeps its own bar.
- **Choosing pages:** under **Einstellungen → Datenseiten**, and the pause menu has the same entry. Each page can be switched on or off. The choice is saved on the watch. At least one page stays on.
- **Charts:** the history has a fixed size (160 points). When it is full, neighbouring points are merged, so a 10-minute run and a 4-hour run both fit.
- **Map:** Connect IQ gives apps no map tiles on most watches, so the app draws the route itself. That works on every watch. There is no planned route to follow, because MaraPlanna has none; the arrow points back to where you started.
- **Finish forecast:** with a session, the ratio of your actual to the planned time so far is applied to the rest of the plan (limited to 0.7-1.5). "On target" means within 1 %, at least 15 s. On race day the target is your goal time. Without a session it is simply the time so far plus the remaining distance at your average pace.
- **Every full kilometer** (every mile if MaraPlanna is set to miles): the watch beeps and vibrates once, and for 8 seconds the page is replaced by a summary of that kilometer: the time (which for exactly 1 km is also the pace), the average heart rate of that kilometer, the average pace of the whole run so far, and the difference to the kilometer before ("VORHER -" in green = faster, "VORHER +" in orange = slower). Any page change hides it at once. The time is interpolated between two one-second readings, so it is exact to the second. A distance reading that jumps back (no GPS fix) is ignored. The splits are only shown, they do not add laps to the recording.
- **Pace alerts:** the pace is smoothed over about 10 s. Outside the target range the watch shows "LANGSAMER" or "SCHNELLER" and vibrates. Alerts come at most every 30 s, and not in the first 20 s of a step.
- **Today's session:** the steps run one after the other: warm-up, every rep, recoveries, cool-down. Distance and time steps switch by themselves and the lap button skips to the next step. **Laps (Runden):** in the recording every step is a lap. Garmin's own spoken lap alert (audio prompts via headphones/phone, set up in Garmin Connect) announces each lap with that lap's values, so only this way the announcement after a rep has the rep's time and pace; the app cannot speak itself and cannot skip single announcements, so after a recovery there is a short announcement too. The app itself counts a rep and the recovery after it as one round: the recovery is shown as "Trabpause 2/3" (the number of its rep). Warm-up, cool-down and blocks without a recovery (e.g. 20 km steady, then 5 km at race pace) are a round each.
- **Summary after every fast part:** when a rep or a block of a long run (every step of type `a`) ends, the watch gives two long vibrations with the interval tone (different from the single pulse of a plain step change) and for 10 seconds the page shows **METER / KM** (distance of that part), **ZEIT**, **Ø PACE** of that part (green in the target range, orange too fast, blue too slow) and **Ø ALLE** (average pace of all fast parts so far); the header says e.g. "Interval 2/3 geschafft". After a jog recovery, warm-up or cool-down there is none. A page change hides it. Connect IQ gives apps no speech output of their own; what is spoken is Garmin's lap alert (see Laps).
- **FIT file:** besides the normal running data, the activity gets `target_pace_min` and `target_pace_max` (record, s/km, 0 = no target), `pace_zone` (record, 0 = free run … 7 = Repetition) and `vdot` (session).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| START | open the menu | start | pause + menu | resume |
| Menu button (long UP) | open the menu | | | |
| BACK | close the app | back to the menu | lap (next step in a session) | menu |

In the menus UP/DOWN (or swiping) scrolls, START (or a tap) chooses, BACK goes back one level.

While recording, swiping right does **not** count as BACK, so it can't trigger a lap by accident. The pause menu offers **Fortsetzen**, **Datenseiten**, **Speichern** and **Verwerfen**. Verwerfen asks for confirmation first. BACK never ends a recording. If the system closes the app during a recording, the run is **saved**: Connect IQ can't show a question at that point, so saving is the only way to lose nothing.

The watch vibrates and beeps on start, pause, lap/step and save, but only if the watch supports it and vibration and tones are switched on.

## Where the paces come from

1. **MaraPlanna (linked watch):** on first start the app shows a 6-digit code. Enter it on the website under **Settings → Garmin watch**. The app then loads your paces, VDOT and today's session through the phone (`GET /watch/run`, see `supabase/functions/_shared/runFeed.ts`). It does this when it starts and again when the home screen opens and the data is more than 15 minutes old, and keeps the data for runs without the phone. The sessions' steps and the finished sessions come in a second, separate request (`GET /watch/plan`, see `supabase/functions/_shared/planFeed.ts`) that follows the first one and is stored under its own key, so the main feed stays small; if it fails (an older function), the lists show title, distance and status only. Besides the paces and today's session the feed carries the week (Monday, kilometres run and planned), the sessions of this and next week (day, title, kilometres, done / planned / skipped) and the goal race (date, name). The week data needs the `watch` Edge Function to be deployed (`npx supabase functions deploy watch --no-verify-jwt`); an older function still works, the week and plan pages then say "Keine Plandaten" and the sessions have no details.
   **Automatic loading in the background** (`source/BgSync.mc`, `source/BgStore.mc`): the watch wakes the app even when it is closed, every morning at **6:30 and then every 30 minutes until 22:00** (not at night, to save battery), and loads today's run with the paces (`/watch/run`) and, on watches with strength, today's strength workouts (`/watch/strength`) through the phone, stored exactly like a normal sync. Opening the app later, the data is already there, even without the phone. Garmin allows one scheduled background task at a time and at least 5 minutes between two, so every run first books the next slot; a slot without the phone just fails and the next one tries again. A run may last 30 s and has little memory: measured in the simulator on the Forerunner 165 with the largest strength feed the server sends (6 workouts, 7.3 KB): 15 of 59 KB after the run feed, 35 of 59 KB at the peak while the strength feed is read. The six 128 KB watches load only the run feed in the background (Forerunner 55: 15 of 27 KB). Each run books the next slot itself (a run at 22:14 booked 6:30). **Einstellungen → Automatisch laden** switches it off; its subtitle shows when the background last loaded ("zuletzt 7:30") or an error code. The app needs the `Background` permission (`manifest.xml`). Test in the simulator: `node tools/mock-watch-server.cjs` (a local stand-in for the server that answers like `watch`, with a different session "Hintergrund 5x600" so the change shows under Training → Heute; `MOCK_BIG=1` sends the largest strength feed), switch off *Settings → Use Device HTTPS Requirements* in the simulator, then run the demo build: it books the first background run 20 s after the start and prints each step with the memory used (`bg: …`).
2. **App settings:** without a link, the paces can be entered in the Garmin Connect app (*Connect IQ apps → MaraPlanna → Settings*), e.g. `5:26-5:59` for a range or `4:57` for a single pace (±5 s). This only works when the app was installed from the Connect IQ Store.
3. **No paces:** only "Freier Lauf", with a hint on the start screen.

Paces in the feed are seconds per km; ranges are `[fast, slow]`, and single values get ±5 s.

## Build

Same setup as the watch face (Connect IQ SDK and Java 11+):

```bash
cd garmin-runapp
node tools/products.cjs        # writes every supported watch into manifest.xml
monkeyc -f monkey.jungle -d fr265 -l 3 -o bin/MaraPlanna.prg -y <path>/developer_key.der
monkeyc -f monkey.jungle -e -r -l 3 -o bin/MaraPlanna.iq -y <path>/developer_key.der
```

`-l 3` is Garmin's strictest type checking; the app builds with it, without warnings, for every supported watch.

## Music and the radio

If music (e.g. Spotify) plays on the watch over Bluetooth headphones, anything that keeps the radio, the CPU or the speaker busy can make it stutter. The app therefore keeps its footprint small:
- **Nothing is switched on when the app opens.** In particular there is no `Sensor.setEnabledSensors` call: Garmin documents it as the control for the ANT+ sensors, and ANT+ shares the 2.4 GHz band with Bluetooth audio. The heart rate comes from the watch itself (the activity session, or the watch's own heart-rate history on the home and ready screens). Only if the activity delivers no heart rate for 20 seconds does the app request the sensor once.
- **GPS starts on the ready screen** (so it is searching before you press start) and stops again if you leave that screen without running. On the home screen and in the menus the GPS is off.
- **One redraw per second** instead of two (the GPS callback no longer asks for a redraw), and anti-aliasing is switched off on the run pages (only the home screen needs it for the logo and icons).
- **Beeps and vibration can be switched off separately** in the settings. Vibration alerts do not touch the music.

## Memory on the 128 KB watches

Forerunner 55, Forerunner 245, Venu Sq, fēnix 6, fēnix 6S and Enduro give an app only 128 KB. The release build (`-r`) is about 88 KB of that, because the memory is mostly the loaded code; the arrays for the charts, the track and the kilometre splits are allocated once with a fixed size. Measured in the simulator on the Forerunner 55 with a release demo build, which carries about 4 KB of demo code that the store build does not have: 97 KB on the home screen, 99 to 108 KB in the menus (the page list with ten switches is the highest), 103 KB while running and 106 KB on the end screens, against a limit of 124 KB. The store build is about 4 KB lower. Keep an eye on this when adding features: the Forerunner 55 is the tightest case. Compiling with `-O 3z` (optimise for size) saves almost nothing.

## Demo build (simulator)

`demo.jungle` builds a variant with sample paces, a sample session (3×1000 m) and a sample week (relative to today) instead of pairing, with its own app id. It also fills in a sample weather (17°, partly cloudy) and a sample VO2max (52) if the simulator gives none; the normal build never does that. An autopilot goes through the menu to the ready screen, start, pages, lap, pause menu, the discard question and save, and prints each step to the console:

```bash
node tools/demo-manifest.cjs
monkeyc -f demo.jungle -d fr265 -o bin/demo.prg -y <path>/developer_key.der
monkeydo bin/demo.prg fr265
```

For moving data in the simulator, use **Simulation → Activity Data**: choose *FIT/GPX Playable File* with a GPX track and press play.

**Run simulation (intervals, long run blocks):** `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools/sim-run.ps1 intervals` (3 × 400 m at 4:00 with 60 s jog) or `... long` (2 km easy, then 1 km at marathon pace). It builds the demo build with `Demo.SIM` set, the autopilot starts today's session, and a simulated runner (`source/SimRun.mc`) runs it four times faster than real time: the true pace is the middle of each step's target (jog 6:30), with 3 s of acceleration and noise, and the watch speed lags like on a real watch (6 s time constant). The console (also `bin/sim-<scenario>.log`) shows every second of the first 25 of each step: true pace, the lagging watch speed and the pace the app shows, plus the lap number, and a `>>>` line with the summary values after every fast part.

## Put it on your watch (sideload)

1. Build `MaraPlanna.prg` for your watch model, e.g. `-d fr265`. The ids are in `manifest.xml`.
2. Connect the watch over USB. On Windows it shows up in Explorer as a drive or a device.
3. Copy the `.prg` into `GARMIN/APPS/`, then unplug the watch.
4. Open the app list on the watch (START button → activities/apps): **MaraPlanna** is there (the activity is named "MaraPlanna Lauf" in Garmin Connect).
5. On first start the app shows the code: enter it on the website under **Settings → Garmin watch**, with the phone nearby and Garmin Connect running.

Limits of a sideloaded app: the settings in the Garmin Connect app (pace entry) and the labels of the custom FIT fields in Garmin Connect only appear for apps installed from the Store. The recording itself (time, distance, pace, heart rate, laps) syncs to Garmin Connect as usual.

**For everyone:** upload `MaraPlanna.iq` in the [Connect IQ developer dashboard](https://apps.garmin.com/developer/) as a new app (*Device App*). After Garmin's review, it is in the Connect IQ Store.


## Krafttraining

Menü **Training**: jede Krafteinheit von heute ist ein eigener Eintrag (Name der Einheit, darunter "Kraft · 3 Übungen"; gibt es mehrere, etwa Oberkörper, Unterkörper und Core, wählt man, welche gemacht wird) oder **Kraft frei**. Die Uhr fragt nichts Persönliches ab: Handgelenk, Gewichtseinheit, Zähl-Art je Übung und die Einheiten von heute kommen aus dem Feed, eingerichtet auf der Website unter **Strength**. Ohne Einrichtung zeigt der Menüpunkt "Auf Website einrichten".

- **Feed (zwei Teile):** Der Laufabgleich (`GET /watch/run`) trägt nur `strength` in Kurzform (`ready`, Handgelenk, Einheit, `auto`, `share`); Connect IQ erlaubt höchstens 8 KB je gespeichertem Wert. Die Kraft-Einheiten kommen eigens von `GET /watch/strength?date=` und liegen auf der Uhr unter `str` (Einheiten, Übungen, Signaturen) und `figs` (Figuren). `str` gilt nur an dem Tag, für den es geladen wurde (`d`).
- **Alle Übungen:** Jede Übung des Katalogs (`supabase/functions/_shared/exerciseCatalog.ts`, 61 Stück) ist auf der Uhr, mit Name, Sätzen, Wiederholungen, Gewicht (aus dem letzten Training), Pause, Zähl-Art und Figur. Die Bibliothek (`strength.ts`, `libraryIdOf`) ordnet jede einer Bewegungsfamilie zu.
- **Übungsbibliothek (Kraft frei):** Die 279 Übungen der Liste (`docs/uebungen/liste.txt`) sind nach Muskelgruppe sortiert (Brust, Rücken, Trapez & Nacken, Schultern, Bizeps, Trizeps, Unterarme, Quadrizeps, Hamstrings, Gesäß & Hüfte, Waden, Bauch & Rumpf, Hüftbeuger, Calisthenics, Ganzkörper, Flexband). **Training → Kraft frei** zeigt oben "Meine Übungen" (die Liste von der Website) und darunter die Gruppen mit der Zahl der Übungen; die Gruppe öffnet die Übungen, die diese Person mit ihren Geräten machen kann (Filter nach Gerät: das macht der Server nach den Geräten aus dem Fragebogen). Wahl einer Übung startet ein freies Training mit ihr. Die Listen holt die Uhr beim Abgleich je Gruppe mit einer eigenen Anfrage (`GET /watch/library?group=`, `Sync.loadNextGroup`, gespeichert unter `lib.<Gruppe>`), weil alle zusammen den Speicher der Uhr sprengen würden. Die Figur einer gewählten Übung gibt es nur, wenn sie zu den Einheiten von heute gehört (nur deren Figuren liegen auf der Uhr).
- **Zähl-Art je Übung:** `auto` (die Uhr zählt aus der Bewegung und fragt nach dem Satz), `assist` (die Uhr versucht es, fragt aber immer; START beginnt und beendet den Satz von Hand, z. B. Maschinen, Kabel, Beinpresse), `time` (Halten wie Plank: START beginnt, die Uhr zählt die Sekunden herunter, gespeichert als Sekunden). Die Zähl-Art steht im Katalog je Übung, nicht auf der Uhr.
- **Ablauf:** START beginnt. In der Pause steht die nächste geplante Übung mit Satz und Ziel und ihre Figur. Beginnt eine Bewegung, zählt die Uhr die Wiederholungen mit (`RepCounter`) und zeigt, welche Übung sie dafür hält ("Kniebeuge ?" mit Fragezeichen, solange sie sich nicht sicher ist). Nach dem Satz kommt eine Karte mit Übung, Wiederholungen und Gewicht. Sie schließt sich nach 5 s von selbst, nur wenn die Übung `auto` ist, die Uhr die geplante erkannt hat und sicher ist (und "Satz selbst abschließen" auf der Website an ist). AUF/AB oder Tippen ändern Gewicht, Wiederholungen und Übung, START bestätigt, BACK verwirft. Hat die Uhr eine andere als die geplante Übung erkannt, steht auf der Karte "erkannt".
- **Erkennung der Übung (`Recognizer.mc`):** Aus dem Satz misst `RepCounter` vier Merkmale (Dauer einer Wiederholung, Ausschlag, vertikaler Anteil, Richtung der Schwerkraft am Handgelenk). Der Erkenner bewertet damit alle Übungen der Einheit: der Plan gibt den Vorrang (nächste geplante Übung, noch offene Sätze, Wiederholung der letzten), die Messung die Passung. Die Passung lernt aus Signaturen, die der Server aus den bestätigten Sätzen des Nutzers errechnet (`sig`, je Handgelenk, ab drei Sätzen je Übung) und aus den bereits bestätigten Sätzen von heute. Ohne gelernte Signatur bleibt eine Übung neutral. Es gibt auch die Annahme "keine davon", damit ein fremder Satz nicht einfach zur nächstbesten Übung wird.
- **Grenzen:** Die Schwellen und Vorrangwerte sind Schätzwerte aus den Beispielbewegungen, nicht an echten Daten geprüft. Ein Handgelenk sieht nicht jede Übung; Maschinen und Halteübungen laufen darum über `assist`/`time`. Die Signaturen entstehen erst aus den ersten echten Sätzen des Nutzers.
- **Pause:** Menütaste (lang AUF) öffnet das Menü (Fortsetzen, Speichern, Verwerfen). START öffnet bei `assist` und `time` den nächsten Satz. BACK in der Pause lässt die geplante Übung aus.
- **Aufzeichnung:** Aktivität Krafttraining, eine Runde je Satz, FIT-Felder `set_reps`, `set_weight`, `set_exercise`. Nach dem Speichern gehen die Sätze an `POST /watch/strength` (bei fehlender Verbindung bleiben sie gespeichert und werden beim nächsten Abgleich gesendet), mit Übung, Quelle (`plan`, `auto`, `pick`) und den Messwerten (`diag`) für die Signaturen.
- **Sensoren:** nur während des Krafttrainings, Beschleunigung und (wo vorhanden) Gyroskop, einmal pro Sekunde geliefert. Keine Töne, nur Vibration, damit Musik über Bluetooth nicht stört.
- **Kleine Uhren:** Auf den sechs Uhren mit 128 KB (`enduro`, `fenix6`, `fenix6s`, `fr245`, `fr55`, `venusq`) ist alles Kraft über die Annotationen `strength`/`nostrength` ausgeschlossen (`monkey.jungle`, `demo.jungle`).
- **Demo-Build:** Beispiel-Feed, vom echten Server-Code erzeugt (`bash tools/dump-strength.sh && node tools/gen-demo-strength.cjs` schreibt `source/DemoStrength.mc`): drei Einheiten von heute (Oberkörper, Unterkörper, Core mit Halteübung) und eine künstliche Handgelenkbewegung. Nur im Demo-Build vorhanden.
- **Tests:** `StrengthTests.mc` (Zähler, Plan, Erkenner, Halten, Figuren) laufen im Simulator: `monkeydo bin/test.prg fr165m /t` (in PowerShell; Git Bash verfälscht `/t`).

### Übungsfigur

Während der Pause zeigt die Uhr eine animierte Figur der nächsten geplanten Übung (Seitenansicht, mit Gerät). Es gibt eine Figur je Übung des Katalogs. Sie läuft nur dort, mit zehn Bildern pro Sekunde, und lässt sich auf der Website unter **Strength** abschalten.

- **Daten:** `supabase/functions/_shared/exerciseMoves.ts` macht aus den Gelenkwinkeln der Webseiten-Bilder (`exerciseArtData.ts`) das Format der Uhr (Schlüsselposen mit Winkeln von Rumpf, Kopf, Armen und Beinen, festgehaltener Punkt, Bildausschnitt) und hängt die Geräte als einfache Formen an (Stange, Hantel, Kugelhantel, Bank, Kasten, Kabel, Band, Scheibe). Zwölf Übungen behalten ihre von Hand abgestimmten Bewegungen (`TUNED`, `exercisePoses.ts`). Der Server schickt nur die Figuren der Einheit von heute (höchstens 14, 6,5 KB) im Feed (`an`, auf der Uhr `figs`); die Uhr kennt keine Übung fest.
- **Zeichnen:** `source/strength/Figure.mc`: Skelett mit Vorwärtskinematik, Körperteile als verjüngte Polygone mit Schattenstreifen, Kleidung, Schuhe, Haare, Geräte hinter und vor der Figur. Antialiasing nur für die Figur.
- **Muskeln:** Die trainierten Muskeln sind rot markiert (Hauptmuskeln kräftig, Nebenmuskeln heller). Der Feed trägt dazu `u` = [Hauptmuskeln, Nebenmuskeln] als Bitmasken (`supabase/functions/_shared/figureMuscles.ts`, 15 Muskeln); die Lage der Muskeln am Körper steht in `OVERLAY` (dieselbe Tabelle in `Figure.mc`). Die Website zeichnet dieselbe Figur mit demselben Code-Aufbau (`supabase/functions/_shared/figureDraw.ts`, `src/components/strength/FigureAnimation.tsx`), `src/lib/figureDraw.test.ts` prüft sie.

### Wechsel-Countdown

Before every step change of a MaraPlanna session the watch gives **five short vibrations, one per second** (5, 4, 3, 2, 1), then the usual lap signal at the change itself. It applies to every transition: warm-up to interval, interval to jog, jog to interval, and also from one block to the next (5 km at pace A, then 5 km at pace B). Nothing is drawn: you feel it. It does not count down before the last step (nothing follows) and not in a free run without steps. It can be switched off under Einstellungen → Wechsel-Countdown; with vibration off (in the app or on the watch) a short tone takes its place, so the countdown is not lost.

- **Time steps** (e.g. 2 min jog) are exact: pulses at 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 s left.
- **Distance steps** (e.g. 1000 m) estimate the time left from the metres to go and the speed of the last four seconds. The estimate only decides when the countdown starts (the change is at most five seconds away); then the beat goes on evenly, one pulse per second, up to five. If you slow down or stop so that the change is far away again, it waits for the next approach. A step shorter than the countdown gets only the part that is left after five seconds in the step.
- `source/StepCountdown.mc` is plain logic (tested), `Workout.secondsLeft` gives the estimate, `RecordingController.tick` asks once per second, `Alert.countdown` vibrates (full strength, 130 ms).

Measured in the simulator against a recorded track with a 14 s time step and a 70 m step: five pulses, one per second, before each change.

### Trainingsplan and Absolviert on the small watches

The six watches with 128 KB of memory (Enduro, fēnix 6 and 6S, Forerunner 245 and 55, Venu Sq) keep the plain pages instead of the lists (the code of the lists and the second request are left out by `monkey.jungle`, `excludeAnnotations`, like the strength part): "Absolviert" opens the week view, "Trainingsplan" the page with the race and the next three sessions. The memory use of the Forerunner 55 with the new version is 98 of 124 kB at the home screen (before: 97 kB); with the lists it would have been 110 kB. Everything else, including the countdown, is the same on all watches. The entry point is `source/PlanEntry.mc`.
